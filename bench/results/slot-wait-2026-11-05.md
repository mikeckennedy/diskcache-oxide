# Slot wait (2026-11-05)

Plan 12 step 5, build-order step 3 (5b): `Slots::acquire_until` (`crates/diskcache-oxide-core/src/genlock.rs`) used to sleep a fixed 50 µs whenever `try_acquire` failed. Now it takes the permit only attached, and its single detached wait watches a plain load (`in_use() < cap`): a spin of `SLOT_SPIN` = 1,024 `spin_loop` hints (gaps doubling to 64), then the old 50 µs sleeps, and it returns for an attached `try_acquire`. The spin is off on 2 or fewer CPUs (the SQLite mutex's rule), and at most half the CPUs' worth of waiters, process-wide, spin at once (`SPINNERS`, generation-packed; at least 1, 4 if the CPU count is unknown); the rest go straight to the sleeps. The Design's yield phase was built, swept and removed (below).

Release wheels, Python 3.14t, 6-byte values, 700 URL-shaped keys, reads only, one shared handle per case. `before` is `55397ec` (5a), `after` is this change. `bench/scaling/slot_wait.py`: each arm in a fresh process, arms interleaved, thousands of gets/s. Sweep arms use a `--cfg dco_spin_sweep` build, which reads `DCO_SLOT_SPIN` and `DCO_SLOT_SPINNERS` (the yield sweeps below used an earlier build that also read `DCO_SLOT_YIELDS`); arms are named `s<spin>y<yields>sp<spinners>`. `fanout8` is `FanoutCache(shards=8)` at its default budget (8 connections, one per shard); `c32` is 32 connections. `Cache`'s default budget is 8 connections, so `cache 32t`/`36t` waits for slots too.

The Mac's load average was 3-10 around the runs (desktop apps and an agent viewer at 75-175% of a core); a first try at this table was discarded because two of its rounds ran under a load of 14.

- **FanoutCache at the default budget, 4 threads:** +46% on the Mac, +11% in the VM (+10% to +19% across the VM sweep runs of the shipped arm, so the VM's gain is noisy). It's still below plain `Cache` (1,384 and 1,486) and well below 32 connections (1,526 and 1,862).
- **Single-thread is unchanged** (within ±3%). The 4-thread `cache` and `c32` rows barely wait for slots and move within noise.
- **2 CPUs:** neutral; the spin is off there, so the wait is the old sleep loop.
- **Oversubscribed:** FanoutCache's default budget gains 25% in the VM and 3% on the Mac. Plain `Cache` loses 7% on the Mac at 36 threads (every after round, 582-617, is below four of five before rounds, 631-692) and 5% in the VM at 32; `c32` loses 6% in the VM and gains 4% on the Mac. The likely reason for the `Cache` loss: with many more threads than its 8 slots, waiters asleep for 50 µs kept fewer connections inside SQLite at once, and SQLite's shared-memory mutex is plain `Cache`'s bottleneck (`read-ceiling-2026-11-05.md`). On the Mac, a load check then sleep (`s0y0`) is the only arm that doesn't lose there. A trade-off, open for Michael (below).

## Tail check

`bench/scaling/slot_tail.py`: 16 threads on one `FanoutCache(8)` at the default budget, 60 s, `durable=False`, `timeout=0.05`, so a slot wait past 50 ms raises. Per-`get` time includes the slot wait, so it bounds it; quantiles are bucket lower bounds (within 12.5%). The Mac's load average was 3.4 at the start.

| Run | k gets/s | Timeouts | p50 | p99 | p99.9 | p99.99 | max |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Mac before | 827 | 0 | 2.3 µs | 197 µs | 328 µs | 492 µs | 1.3 ms |
| Mac after | 918 | 0 | 4.6 µs | 328 µs | 786 µs | 1.3 ms | 3.8 ms |
| VM 8 CPUs before | 818 | 0 | 0.96 µs | 3.8 µs | 3.1 ms | 6.3 ms | 24 ms |
| VM 8 CPUs after | 1,255 | 0 | 2.3 µs | 10 µs | 3.1 ms | 11.5 ms | 48 ms |

No `Timeout` at 50 ms, so by the design's rule no `starving` guard is added. Barging shows in the Mac's tail: p99.9 goes from 328 to 786 µs and the worst wait from 1.3 to 3.8 ms, with throughput up 11%. In the VM (16 threads on 8 CPUs, where the tail is preemption) p99.9 is unchanged and throughput is up 53%; the worst wait, 48 ms, is close to the 50 ms timeout. An earlier run with the yields and `timeout=1.0` (Mac load 4-5) gave the same picture: Mac p99.9 328 against 786 µs, max 1.6 against 10 ms; VM 3.1 against 3.4 ms.

## Open

- **Plain `Cache` oversubscribed: -7% on the Mac at 36 threads, -5% in the VM at 32.** A faster slot hand-off lets more threads into SQLite at once. Options: accept it (36 busy reader threads on one `Cache` is far past its 8 connections), or skip the spin when the waiters far outnumber `cap`. Not measured on x86-64 (htz) yet, as with 5a's budget.
- **FanoutCache's default budget (M2):** after this change it reads 15-25% slower than plain `Cache` at 4 threads, and 23-40% slower than 32 connections. The slot wait no longer explains that gap: with one connection per shard, colliding threads still wait out the holder's whole read.
